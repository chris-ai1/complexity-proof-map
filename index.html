How to read it

Click a node or an edge

Arrows point from what you have to what you get: A → B means a certificate of A yields one of B. Edge labels name the bridge lemma; thicker edges are used more often. Thick green edges are the routes the library recommends.

The polynomial-time hub ComputableInPolyTime sits in the middle: almost every other route ends there, and it feeds the probabilistic (IsPPTOn) and class-level results.

One primary certificate per function (binary ℕ)

A new pattern under #608: each basic function on binary naturals (encNat, least significant bit first) gets one primary certificate, a plain ComputableInTimeAndSpace (or ComputableInO) with explicit bounds, built from a finite-state machine. The linear-time, degree, polynomial-time, space and tidy facades keep their statements but are derived from it. The quadratic folds they replace, and their helpers, are deleted. Before, two-operand functions were only quadratic, because no combinator moved a work head in lockstep with the input.

The tidy → plain migration

Historically, closure rules were proved for tidy machines (which leave their tapes clean), and plain results were obtained by converting back and forth. Issue #862 deletes the tidy calculus, keeping tidiness only as a machine-level kernel. Since 2026-10-28 the tidy surface shrank from 876 to 704 public statements, and only 65 of them remain outside the machine layer (was 91). Proofs outside Machines now convert through .tidy / .of_tidy about 1,200 times, down from about 5,100. The complexity-class proofs, the PCP and crypto clients, GenericRAM simulation, space and probabilistic modules were moved to plain proofs (#1541–#1550, #1591, #1593, #1604, #1610, #1621, #1624, #1632), with new plain rules for degree bounds, space and oracle PPT (#1551, #1558, #1560, #1592, #1601). Each slice moves clients to plain proofs, adds missing plain rules (#608) and deletes tidy declarations once unused (open: #1668, #1670, #1673).
