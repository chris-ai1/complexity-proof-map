How to read it

Click a node or an edge

Arrows point from what you have to what you get: A → B means a certificate of A yields one of B. Edge labels name the bridge lemma; thicker edges are used more often. Thick green edges are the routes the library recommends.

The polynomial-time hub ComputableInPolyTime sits in the middle: almost every other route ends there, and it feeds the probabilistic (IsPPTOn) and class-level results.

The tidy → plain migration

Historically, closure rules were proved for tidy machines (which leave their tapes clean), and plain results were obtained by converting back and forth. Issue #862 deletes the tidy calculus, keeping tidiness only as a machine-level kernel. Statements are already mostly plain (91 public tidy statements remain outside the machine layer); proofs still convert through .tidy / .of_tidy several thousand times. Each slice moves clients to plain proofs, adds missing plain rules (#608) and deletes tidy declarations once unused.
